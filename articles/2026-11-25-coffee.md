*Odkiaľ pochádza káva a ako sa zo zeleného zrnka stane voňavá hnedá káva v šálke.*

Coffee comes from a small red fruit. People call it a coffee **cherry**. The fruit grows on a small tree. Inside each cherry, there are two **seeds**. We call these seeds coffee **beans**.

The beans are green at first, and they don't smell like coffee. Then people **roast** them. They **heat** the beans in a big hot machine. The beans turn brown, and they get the coffee smell we all know. Light roast beans are light brown. Dark roast beans are almost black.

Coffee plants need warm weather and a lot of rain. They don't like cold winters. That is why coffee grows in hot countries, like Brazil, Colombia, Vietnam, and Ethiopia. Brazil grows more coffee than any other country in the world.

Many Americans drink coffee every morning. Some drink it black, with no milk and no sugar. Others add milk, cream, or sugar. In the U.S., people often buy coffee "to go." They get a paper cup and drink it in the car or on the way to work. A small coffee in an American coffee shop is often bigger than a big coffee in Europe!

Americans also have a nice word: a **refill**. In many American restaurants, the waiter fills your cup again, and you don't pay more.

### New words

- **cherry** – čerešňa (tu: plod kávovníka)
- **seed** / seeds – semienko / semienka
- **bean** / beans – zrnko / zrnká (aj fazuľa)
- **roast** – pražiť
- **heat** – zohriať, zahrievať
- **refill** – dolievanie, doplnenie (šálky)

### Useful phrases

- *turn brown* – zhnednúť (turn + farba = zmeniť sa na tú farbu)
- *black coffee* – čierna káva (bez mlieka a cukru)
- *coffee to go* – káva so sebou
- *on the way to work* – cestou do práce
- *That is why…* – Preto…

### Grammar

**There is / there are** = je / sú, nachádza sa / nachádzajú sa.
*There is* + jedna vec, *there are* + viac vecí: *Inside each cherry, there are two seeds.*
