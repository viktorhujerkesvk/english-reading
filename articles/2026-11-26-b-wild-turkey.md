*Morku poznáme z taniera, ale v amerických lesoch žijú milióny divokých moriakov. A tie vedia prekvapiť.*

Turkeys are the stars of Thanksgiving dinner. But not all turkeys live on farms. In the forests of North America, there are millions of **wild** turkeys.

Wild turkeys are big birds. A male turkey is about one meter tall, and he is very proud of his **feathers**. In spring, he opens his tail like a big **fan** and walks slowly in front of the females. He also makes a loud sound: "gobble, gobble." That is why Americans sometimes call a male turkey a "gobbler."

Farm turkeys are heavy, and they can't fly. Wild turkeys are different. They are fast and **strong**. They can run about 40 kilometers per hour, and they can fly short distances. At night, they don't sleep on the ground. They fly up into the trees and sleep on high branches. There, they are safe from foxes and other **enemies**.

Wild turkeys eat almost everything: seeds, nuts, berries, insects, and even small frogs. They look for food on the ground in small groups. A group of turkeys is called a **flock**.

Many people don't know this, but turkeys have very good eyes. They see colors well, and they can see a very wide area without moving their heads. It is hard to surprise a wild turkey!

### New words

- **wild** – divoký
- **feather** / feathers – pierko / perie
- **fan** – vejár (aj ventilátor, aj fanúšik)
- **strong** – silný
- **enemy** / enemies – nepriateľ / nepriatelia
- **flock** – kŕdeľ, stádo (vtákov alebo oviec)

### Useful phrases

- *be proud of…* – byť hrdý na…
- *in front of…* – pred (miestom)
- *per hour* – za hodinu
- *is called…* – volá sa…
- *look for…* – hľadať…
