*Deň po Thanksgivingu je Black Friday – deň veľkých zliav. Prečo sa volá „čierny“ piatok?*

Today is Black Friday. It is the day after Thanksgiving, and in the U.S. it is the start of the Christmas **shopping** season.

On Black Friday, many stores have big **sales**. Prices are lower than usual, sometimes 50 percent lower or more. Some stores open very early in the morning, and people wait outside in long **lines**. They want to be the first inside. Some people only want a new TV. Others buy all their Christmas gifts in one day.

Why "black"? There are two stories. The first story is about traffic. The day after Thanksgiving, the streets are full of cars and **crowds** of people. For police officers in Philadelphia, it is a "black" day – a long, hard, and tiring day.

The second story is about money. Stores write their numbers in a book. Red numbers mean the store loses money. Black numbers mean the store makes money. On Black Friday, many stores go from red to black.

Today, Black Friday is not only American. Stores in Europe, and also in Slovakia, have Black Friday sales too. And there is another day: Cyber Monday. On the Monday after Thanksgiving, online stores have big sales. Many people stay home and shop on their phones.

A good tip: before you buy something on Black Friday, check the **price** a few weeks earlier. Sometimes the "sale" price is not really a good **deal**.

### New words

- **shopping** – nakupovanie
- **sale** / sales – zľava, výpredaj (aj predaj)
- **line** – rad (ľudí); Briti hovoria *queue*
- **crowd** / crowds – dav / davy
- **price** – cena
- **deal** – obchod, výhodná kúpa

### Useful phrases

- *the day after…* – deň po…
- *wait in line* – stáť v rade
- *lower than usual* – nižší ako zvyčajne
- *make money / lose money* – zarábať / prerábať
- *a good deal* – výhodná kúpa
