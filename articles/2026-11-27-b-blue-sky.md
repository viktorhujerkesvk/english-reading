title: Why Is the Sky Blue?
publish: 2026-11-27 17:00

*Prečo je obloha cez deň modrá, ale pri západe slnka červená a oranžová?*

On a sunny day, the sky is blue. But why? Sunlight looks white, but it is really a **mix** of all colors: red, orange, yellow, green, blue, and violet.

The light from the sun travels to Earth. Before it **reaches** us, it goes through the air. Air is not empty. It is full of very **tiny** pieces called molecules. You can't see them, but they are everywhere.

When sunlight hits these molecules, something interesting happens. Red and yellow light go through the air easily. But blue light hits the molecules and **bounces** in all directions. It **spreads** across the whole sky. So when we look up, we see blue light from every direction, and the sky looks blue.

What about **sunsets**? In the evening, the sun is low. Its light travels a much longer way through the air. On this long way, most of the blue light spreads out and disappears. Only the red and orange light gets to our eyes. That is why sunsets are red and orange.

And why isn't the sky violet? There is some violet light too, but there is less of it in sunlight, and our eyes are not very good at seeing it. So for us, the sky is simply blue.

### New words

- **mix** – zmes; miešať
- **reach** – dosiahnuť, dostať sa k
- **tiny** – maličký, drobný
- **bounce** – odraziť sa (ako lopta)
- **spread** – rozšíriť sa, rozprestrieť sa
- **sunset** – západ slnka

### Useful phrases

- *in all directions* – všetkými smermi
- *go through…* – prejsť cez…
- *be good at…* – byť dobrý v niečom
- *What about…?* – A čo…?

### Review
