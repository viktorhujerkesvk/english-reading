*Ako vzniká sneh a je naozaj pravda, že nie sú dve rovnaké snehové vločky?*

Snow starts high in the clouds. Clouds are full of tiny drops of water. When the air in a cloud is very cold, below zero degrees Celsius, the water **freezes**.

A snowflake starts as a very small piece of **ice**. Often there is a tiny piece of **dust** in the middle. Water from the cloud freezes onto it, and the ice grows. Ice crystals have six **sides**, so most snowflakes have six arms.

As the snowflake falls, it goes through air with different temperatures and different amounts of water. Every change makes the arms grow in a **slightly** different way. That is why people say that no two snowflakes are the same. Nobody can check all of them, of course, but scientists think it is true.

Snowflakes are usually small, but sometimes many flakes **stick** together. Then big, soft flakes fall from the sky, like pieces of cotton.

Snow is also **quiet**. Fresh snow has a lot of air between the flakes, and this air takes in sound. After a big snowfall, a city is often much quieter than usual.

In the U.S., some places get a lot of snow. In parts of New York State, near the Great Lakes, there is sometimes more than one meter of snow in one day. Americans call this "lake-effect snow."

### New words

- **freeze** – zamrznúť
- **ice** – ľad
- **dust** – prach
- **side** / sides – strana / strany
- **slightly** – trochu, mierne
- **stick** – lepiť sa, prilepiť sa
- **quiet** – tichý

### Useful phrases

- *below zero* – pod nulou
- *stick together* – zlepiť sa
- *No two snowflakes are the same.* – Žiadne dve vločky nie sú rovnaké.
- *take in sound* – pohlcovať zvuk
