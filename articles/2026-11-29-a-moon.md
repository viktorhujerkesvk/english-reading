*Prečo Mesiac mení tvar a prečo z neho vidíme stále tú istú stranu?*

The Moon is our closest **neighbor** in space. It is about 384,000 kilometers from Earth. A car at 100 kilometers per hour needs about five months to get there – with no stops!

The Moon doesn't make its own light. It **reflects** light from the sun, like a big **mirror**. The sun always lights one half of the Moon. As the Moon goes around Earth, we see different parts of this light half. That is why the Moon changes its **shape**. We call these shapes the phases of the Moon: new moon, half moon, full moon, and back again. One full circle takes about 29 days.

Here is an interesting fact: we always see the same side of the Moon. The Moon turns around once while it goes around Earth once. So the same face always looks at us. Nobody on Earth can see the far side of the Moon without a spaceship.

The Moon is also important for the sea. Its gravity **pulls** on the water of the oceans. This makes the water go up and down every day. We call this the **tide**. At the beach, you can see the tide come in and go out.

Some people say the Moon is made of cheese. That is just a joke, of course. It is made of rock and dust, and it has no air, no rivers, and no lakes.

### New words

- **neighbor** – sused (americky; Briti píšu *neighbour*)
- **reflect** – odrážať
- **mirror** – zrkadlo
- **shape** – tvar
- **pull** – ťahať, priťahovať
- **tide** – príliv a odliv

### Useful phrases

- *go around…* – obiehať okolo…
- *full moon / new moon* – spln / nov
- *the tide comes in / goes out* – príliv / odliv
- *be made of…* – byť (vyrobený) z…
