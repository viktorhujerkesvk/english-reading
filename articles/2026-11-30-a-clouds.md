*Z čoho sú oblaky, prečo nepadajú a prečo sú niekedy biele a inokedy tmavé?*

Clouds look light and soft, but they are made of water. A cloud is millions of tiny drops of water or tiny pieces of ice. They are so small and light that they **float** in the air.

How does a cloud start? The sun warms the ground, and warm air goes up. Warm air has water in it, but it is a gas, and we can't see it. High in the sky, the air is colder. There, the gas turns into tiny drops. Many drops together make a cloud.

But clouds are not as light as they look. A small white cloud **weighs** about 500 tons. That is as **heavy** as about 100 elephants! It doesn't fall because the water spreads over a very large area, and the drops are tiny.

Why are some clouds white and others dark gray? White clouds are **thin**, and sunlight goes through them. Rain clouds are **thick** and full of big drops. Light can't get through them easily, so from below, they look dark. When you see dark gray clouds, take an **umbrella**!

Clouds have names. Big, soft white clouds on a nice day are called "cumulus." Thin, high clouds that look like white hair are "cirrus." Some people like to lie on the grass and find shapes in the clouds: a dog, a ship, or a face.

### New words

- **float** – vznášať sa, plávať (na hladine)
- **weigh** – vážiť
- **heavy** – ťažký
- **thin** – tenký
- **thick** – hrubý, hustý
- **umbrella** – dáždnik

### Useful phrases

- *be made of…* – byť z…
- *as heavy as…* – rovnako ťažký ako…
- *not as light as they look* – nie také ľahké, ako vyzerajú
- *gray* – sivý (americky; Briti píšu *grey*)

### Review
