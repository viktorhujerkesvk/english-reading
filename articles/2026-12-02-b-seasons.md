title: Why Do We Have Seasons?
publish: 2026-12-02 17:00

*Prečo máme jar, leto, jeseň a zimu? Nie je to tým, že je Zem raz bližšie a raz ďalej od Slnka.*

Many people think we have summer because Earth is closer to the sun. It sounds logical, but it is not true. In fact, Earth is closest to the sun in early January – in the middle of our winter!

The real **reason** is that Earth is not straight. It **leans** to one side a little, about 23 degrees. Earth goes around the sun once a year, but it always leans in the same direction.

So for part of the year, the northern half of Earth leans **toward** the sun. The sun is high in the sky, the days are long, and the sunlight is strong. That is our summer. Half a year later, the northern half leans away from the sun. The sun is low, the days are short, and the light is **weak**. That is our winter. Spring and fall are in between.

When it is winter in Slovakia, it is summer in Australia. Many Australians **celebrate** Christmas on the beach, with a barbecue instead of a warm dinner at home.

Near the equator, the **seasons** are not very different. It is warm all year. But near the North Pole, the differences are huge. In summer, the sun doesn't go down for weeks. In winter, it doesn't come up for weeks.

By the way, Americans usually say "fall," not "autumn." The name comes from the leaves that fall from the trees. Easy to remember!

### New words

- **reason** – dôvod
- **lean** – nakláňať sa, opierať sa
- **toward** – smerom k (americky; Briti píšu aj *towards*)
- **weak** – slabý
- **celebrate** – oslavovať
- **season** / seasons – ročné obdobie (aj sezóna)

### Useful phrases

- *in fact* – v skutočnosti
- *in between* – medzi tým
- *instead of…* – namiesto…
- *the sun goes down / comes up* – slnko zapadá / vychádza
- *by the way* – mimochodom
